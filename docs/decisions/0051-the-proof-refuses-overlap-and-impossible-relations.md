# 0051. The proof decides from the admitted orderings and the operands' intervals whether a union is exclusive and whether a relation can ever hold or ever refuse

Status: accepted

## Context

A choice is a union whose members each say fully what they are (0003). Two members that can both construct from one scope would make Pydantic's smart mode the decision, silently, at run time. A relation whose operands' intervals make it certain or impossible is a rule that decides nothing. Whatever the proof refuses must be refused soundly: a program it refuses must have the fault.

## Rejected

Leaving disjointness to the developer's discipline and a test. A list of overlap rules in the engine, one per pair of kinds: it held the kinds' meanings in the engine, and it was wrong, since it called two relations of different kinds disjoint while `Equal` implies `AtMost`. A run-time refusal when two members are both present: a check on data that exists, made necessary only by a proof that stopped short. Refusing a pair as overlapping because an over-approximation of their constraints was feasible: feasibility of an over-approximation proves nothing, so that refusal was unsound. Treating a derived value as unbounded, so that no relation over one could be decided. Tolerating a relation that can never refuse: a declaration with no content.

## Decision

Three reads, each from the declarations. The interval of a numeric class, `interval(cls)`: a constant is its default exactly (0057); a scalar is its `gt`, `ge`, `lt` and `le` metadata on `root`, a scalar over `int` reading its strict bounds as the next integer's non-strict ones; a derivation is what its `Derived.interval` makes of its operands' intervals (0047); anything else is unbounded. The orderings two intervals allow, `outcomes(left, right)`: -1 when the first's low is under the second's high, 1 when the second's low is under the first's high, 0 when the two meet. Whether a set of classes can all be present in one scope, `conflict(present)`: not when two of them decide against each other, a pair of records one action hears, two relations whose comparisons over the same operands admit disjoint orderings, with the second's negated when its operands are the first's reversed, or two selections over one element class with disjoint counts; and not when the intervals of its numeric classes and the comparisons of its relations, as difference constraints, have no solution, which Bellman-Ford decides exactly. Infeasibility of an over-approximation is sound: no real values satisfy it either.

A relation never holds when some comparison it makes admits none of the orderings its operands' intervals allow, and never refuses when every comparison admits every ordering they allow; the proof refuses both as `never`. Two classes a union field may hold are exclusive when every choice of what each holds, through its single fields and their unions, conflicts with every choice of the other; a pair not proven exclusive is refused as `ambiguous`, since a union the proof cannot decide is one the run would decide. The proof never refuses a pair it has shown can both hold nothing of; it refuses what it cannot prove, and says so in the message.

## Evidence

With `SmallAmount` bounded `gt=0, le=100` and `LargeAmount` bounded `gt=100`, `outcomes` of their intervals was `{-1}`: `Impossible(Beyond[SmallAmount, LargeAmount])` was refused as `never` "never holds, by the intervals of its operands", `Certain(AtMost[SmallAmount, LargeAmount])` as "never refuses", and `Never(Equal[SmallAmount, LargeAmount])` as "never holds"; `Undecided(Near[Amount, Limit])`, a relation kind with no comparison, as "never refuses". `interval(Amount)` with `gt=0` was `Interval(low=Decimal(0), low_strict=True)`, `interval(Share)` with `gt=0, le=1` was `Interval(low=Decimal(0), low_strict=True, high=Decimal(1))`, `interval(Limit)` was `Interval(low=Decimal(10), high=Decimal(10))`, and `outcomes(interval(Amount), interval(Limit))` was `{-1, 0, 1}`, so `Low` and `High` are neither certain nor impossible. `Unit(Scalar[int])` with `gt=0, lt=2` read as `Interval(low=Decimal(1), high=Decimal(1))`, and `Same(Equal[Unit, One])` with `One` at 1 was read as never refusing. `coexist(Cheap, Dear)`, told apart by `Low(AtMost[Amount, Limit])` and `High(Beyond[Amount, Limit])`, was false, by the partition of the orderings; `coexist(AtOrUnder, Exactly)`, told apart by `AtMost` and `Equal` over the same operands, was true, and `Either.which: AtOrUnder | Exactly` was refused as `ambiguous` at `("Either", "which")`. `Spoken.which: Echo | Alike`, two records one action hears, was not refused.

## Consequences

A wrong union is unwritable, and a union the proof cannot decide is unwritable too; a relation that decides nothing is unwritable. A bound on a scalar, a constant's value and an operation's interval are each read by the proof, so a derived value is decided from what it is derived from. Refuted by a pair the proof refuses as ambiguous whose members cannot both hold; that is a conflict the reads do not express, and the read is added.
